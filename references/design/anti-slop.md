# Design Rules

Read before designing anything.

Models drift toward the average of everything they have seen. In design that
average has a look, and people recognise it instantly as machine-made. Avoiding
it takes deliberate effort on every deck.

---

## The tells

If a deck has these, it looks generated:

- **Purple or blue gradient on white.** The single most recognisable tell.
- **Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, Arial, or the system font stack.** Competent and
  anonymous.
- **Everything in rounded cards with soft grey shadows.** The dashboard look,
  applied to content that is not a dashboard.
- **Three columns of equal weight** for content that is not three equal things.
- **An icon above every heading**, usually generic and adding nothing.
- **Evenly distributed colour**, five tones at similar strength, no hierarchy.
- **Centred everything.** Centring is a decision, not a default.
- **Gradient text** on a headline.
- **Emoji as section markers.**

Any two of these together and the deck reads as generated regardless of how good
the content is.

---

## Instead

**Commit to a palette.** One dominant surface, one sharp accent, one or two
neutrals. The accent appears rarely and means something: this number matters,
this is the recommendation. A palette where everything is coloured has no
emphasis left.

**Choose type with a point of view.** A serif with real character, a grotesque
with tight spacing, a mono for data. Pair a display face with a body face and let
them differ. If the font choice would suit any deck about anything, choose again.

**Build atmosphere, not flat fill.** A subtle grid, a paper texture, a single
large graphic device bleeding off the edge, a photograph under a colour wash. The
reference deck used a dashed route line threading every slide. One idea, repeated,
becomes a signature.

**Let hierarchy be obvious.** The most important thing on a slide should be
three or four times the size of the least important. Timid hierarchy reads as
indecision.

**Break the grid once.** A single element that crosses a column boundary or
bleeds off the edge tells the eye a person made this.

---

## Fit the occasion

The deck should look like it was made for this specific situation.

| Setting | Direction |
|---|---|
| Board or investors | Restrained, high contrast, serif, sparing colour |
| Conference stage | Huge type, dark surface, few words, strong images |
| Client deliverable | Structured, sourced, light surface, quietly authoritative |
| Internal update | Plain, fast to read, no ceremony |
| Campaign or creative | Expressive, confident colour, real work shown large |
| Teaching | Calm, generous spacing, consistent, built to revisit |

A pitch deck that looks like a lecture fails, and so does the reverse.

---

## Where the room changes the design

Ask where it will be shown, because it constrains real choices.

- **Projector.** Washes out contrast and eats dark tones. Light surface with dark
  text survives a bad projector. A dark deck may not.
- **Large screen or TV.** Anything works. Dark decks look excellent.
- **Laptop, shared over video.** Compression destroys fine detail and subtle
  gradients. Go bolder than feels necessary.
- **Printed or forwarded as PDF.** Light surface. Dark decks cost a fortune to
  print and often get printed anyway.

---

## Contrast

Not decoration. Someone at the back of the room has to read it.

- Body text against its background: 4.5:1 minimum
- Large text, 24px and above at stage size: 3:1 minimum
- Never carry meaning in colour alone. Add a label, an icon, or a position.

Muted secondary text is fine for captions and source lines. It is not fine for
anything the audience has to read.

---

## Before calling it done

1. Would this look right if a designer made it for this exact occasion?
2. Is there one visual idea that recurs, or is every slide solving itself alone?
3. Could the font choice belong to any deck about anything?
4. Is the accent colour doing a job, or just present?
5. Does the most important thing on each slide look most important?
6. Does anything on the tells list appear?

If the honest answer to the first is no, the deck is not finished, however clean
it looks.
