# Internal Updates

Team updates, project reviews, weekly and monthly reporting, status to leadership.

Nobody in the room chose to be there and everyone has read a hundred of these.
Respect that by being fast.

---

## The shape

| Slide | Carries |
|---|---|
| **Where we are** | One slide. Green, amber or red, and why |
| **What changed** | Since the last update only. Not the whole history |
| **The numbers** | The three or four that matter, against target |
| **What is blocked** | What you need, from whom, by when |
| **What is next** | The next period, with owners |

Five to twelve slides. If it is longer than twelve, it is a document. Send the
document.

---

## Lead with the status

The first slide says whether things are fine. Everything else is explanation.

Burying the status until slide six wastes the attention of everyone who only
needed that one answer. People who want detail will keep reading.

Say the uncomfortable thing first. An update that hides a problem until the end
teaches people to read your updates suspiciously.

---

## Only what changed

The most common failure is restating context everyone already has. Last month's
numbers, the project background, the team structure, the goals. They know.

Assume the audience read the last update. If they did not, that is what the link
is for.

---

## Blockers need a name and a date

> ✗ We are facing some resource constraints
> ✓ We need one backend engineer for three weeks. Without a decision by Friday,
>   the launch moves to November.

A blocker without an owner and a date is a complaint. A blocker with both is a
decision someone can make in the room.

---

## Numbers against target

A number alone means nothing. Show it against the target, the previous period, or
both.

> ✗ 1,240 signups
> ✓ 1,240 signups, against a target of 1,500. Down from 1,610 last month.

Use the stat block for three or fewer figures. Use a small line chart when the
trend matters more than the value.

---

## Voice

Direct and unceremonious. No preamble, no thanking everyone for their time on a
slide, no restating the agenda.

This is the deck type where filler vocabulary is most tempting and most obvious.
Nobody internally is impressed by "leveraging cross-functional synergies". Say
who did what.

Contractions are fine. Short sentences. Bullets can be fragments.

---

## Design

Plain. This is the one deck type where visual ambition is a liability. Effort
spent on the design of a weekly update reads as effort not spent on the work.

Use `Clinic` or `Blueprint` from the presets. Light background, high contrast,
fast to scan, prints legibly.

---

## Common failures

- **Restating context everyone has.**
- **Burying the status.** It belongs on slide one.
- **Blockers with no owner or date.**
- **Numbers with no target or comparison.**
- **Turning a status update into a presentation.** If nobody needs to discuss it,
  send a message instead.
