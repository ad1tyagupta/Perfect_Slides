# Scenario Sequences

Proven slide orders for specific situations, taken from professional templates.

These are not frameworks. Pick the closest framework from `index.md` for
structure, density and voice, then use the sequence here as the outline.

When a request matches one of these exactly, use it. When it is close, adapt
rather than forcing it.

---

## Quarterly business review

Framework: `internal.md`. 12 to 16 slides.

1. Title, quarter and owner
2. Executive summary, the quarter in four lines
3. Performance against target
4. Business milestones
5. Revenue by source
6. Productivity or operational metrics
7. Cash position
8. What we improved, and the effect
9. Headwinds we hit
10. Priorities for next quarter
11. Next steps, with owners
12. Close

Lead with whether the quarter was good. Everything after is explanation.

## Competitor analysis

Framework: `research.md`. 10 to 14 slides.

1. Title and scope, which competitors and why these
2. Market trends shaping the category
3. Target audience, who everyone is fighting over
4. Direct competitors, the set
5. Competitor profile, one slide each for the top two or three
6. Feature or capability comparison, as a table
7. Pricing comparison
8. Positioning map, a 2x2
9. Where we win
10. Where we are exposed
11. Opportunities
12. Threats
13. Recommended response

Name real weaknesses of your own. A competitor analysis where you win on every
axis is not analysis.

## Customer personas

Framework: `campaign.md`. 6 to 10 slides.

1. Title
2. What a persona is here, and what it is built from
3. Persona one, full profile
4. Persona two, full profile
5. Decision-making journey, how they actually buy
6. What this changes about our strategy

Two personas. Use the tab switcher so both sit on one slide in HTML. Each
profile carries a real quote, what they use today, and what would make them
switch.

## OKR presentation

Framework: `internal.md`. 8 to 12 slides.

1. Title, the period
2. What we are optimising for this quarter
3. Company objectives
4. Objective one, with its key results
5. Objective two, with its key results
6. Objective three, with its key results
7. The single number that matters most
8. What we are deliberately not doing
9. Next quarter preview

Every key result is a number with a baseline and a date. "Improve onboarding" is
not a key result. The "not doing" slide is what makes the list credible.

## Case study

Framework: `campaign.md` for a sales audience, `research.md` for an internal one.
10 to 14 slides.

1. Title, client and outcome in one line
2. The client, briefly
3. The headline result, one number, full slide
4. Supporting results, two or three metrics
5. The situation before
6. The problem, stated properly
7. What we proposed
8. What we actually did
9. Results, with the timeline
10. What the client said, a real quote
11. What we would do differently
12. Where this applies next

Put the outcome on slide three, not slide eleven. Nobody reads a case study to
be surprised.

## Sales deck

Framework: `pitch-deck.md`. 12 to 16 slides.

1. Title
2. The problem, in the buyer's words
3. Why the usual fix does not work
4. What we do
5. How it works, three steps
6. Proof, a case study or number
7. Who else uses this
8. Pricing
9. Objections, answered directly
10. What onboarding looks like
11. Competitive comparison
12. Next step, one specific action

The objections slide is the one most sales decks omit and the one buyers most
want. Answer the two you always get.

## Business proposal

Framework: `pitch-deck.md`. 8 to 12 slides.

1. Title, client and project
2. Our understanding of the problem
3. Proposed approach
4. Scope, what is in and explicitly what is out
5. Deliverables
6. Timeline with milestones
7. Team and who does what
8. Budget
9. Why us
10. Next step and decision date

The out-of-scope line prevents the argument that otherwise happens in month
three. Put it on the slide, not in a footnote.

## Project or change plan

Framework: `internal.md`. 10 to 14 slides.

1. Title
2. Why we are doing this
3. What changes, specifically
4. Who is affected, and how
5. Phases and timeline
6. Roles and owners
7. Risks and mitigations
8. What support people get
9. How we will know it worked
10. Decisions needed today

For change management, the "who is affected and how" slide does most of the
work. Write it from the affected person's side, not the organisation's.

---

## Annual or impact report

Framework: `consulting-roadshow.md`. 12 to 18 slides.

1. Title, the year
2. Opening statement from leadership
3. The year in numbers, three to five figures
4. Highlights, one per slide
5. Deep dive on the biggest programme
6. Financials
7. What did not go to plan
8. Next year
9. Thanks and credits

The honest slide about what did not work is what makes the rest believable.
