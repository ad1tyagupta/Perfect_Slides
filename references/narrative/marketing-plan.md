# Marketing Plans

The strategy for a quarter or a year, presented to a team or a client for
approval. Objectives, audiences, channels, budget, measurement.

Not a campaign. A campaign is one idea shown large, and that is `campaign.md`.
A plan covers the whole function, and its job is to get resourced.

**Density: 40 to 70 words a slide.** General business register. A headline, a
chart or a structure, and a short interpretation.

---

## The sequence

Fourteen beats. Fourteen to twenty slides.

| # | Slide | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title** | Plan name, period, owner |
| 2 | **Executive summary** | The whole plan on one page. Written last |
| 3 | **Current situation** | Where we are, with last period's numbers |
| 4 | **Market and customer insights** | What changed outside the company |
| 5 | **Marketing objectives** | What this plan must achieve, in numbers |
| 6 | **Target audience** | Who, specifically. Two personas at most |
| 7 | **Positioning and key message** | What we say, and why it lands |
| 8 | **Strategy and pillars** | The two to four bets everything ladders to |
| 9 | **Channels and tactics** | Where the work happens |
| 10 | **Campaign or content plan** | What actually ships, and when |
| 11 | **Budget and resources** | Money and people, by pillar |
| 12 | **Timeline and responsibilities** | Who owns what, by when |
| 13 | **KPIs and measurement** | How we will know, and when we check |
| 14 | **Next steps** | What you need approved today |

---

## The executive summary decides whether the rest gets read

Written last, and the hardest slide. If it cannot be written, the plan is not
finished.

It carries: where we are, what we will do, what it costs, what it returns. Four
lines. Someone who reads only this slide should be able to approve or reject.

---

## Objectives are numbers with dates

The slide most plans fluff.

> ✗ Increase brand awareness and drive engagement
> ✓ 1,500 qualified leads by 31 March, up from 940. Cost per lead under £40.

Every objective needs a baseline, a target and a date. Without a baseline nobody
can tell whether the target is ambitious or trivial.

Three to five objectives. More and none of them are priorities.

---

## Pillars, not a list of tactics

The difference between a plan and a to-do list.

A pillar is a bet: *"win on comparison searches"*, *"turn existing customers
into a referral channel"*. Each tactic underneath ladders to one pillar. Any
tactic that ladders to nothing gets cut, and that is the point of having pillars.

Two to four. Show them as columns, with the tactics beneath each.

---

## Budget by pillar, not by channel

A budget split across channels tells you what you are spending. Split across
pillars, it tells you what you are betting on, and whether the spend matches the
priority you just claimed.

If your biggest pillar has the smallest budget, the plan says one thing and the
money says another. That is a slide worth having, not hiding.

Show cost per outcome, not only total spend.

---

## Measurement includes the check dates

Not just what you measure. When you look, and what you do if it is off.

> Weekly: pipeline and cost per lead. Monthly: full funnel against target.
> If cost per lead exceeds £50 for two consecutive weeks, we pause paid search
> and reallocate to content.

A plan that names its own failure condition is far more credible than one that
assumes success.

---

## Voice

Direct and businesslike. This is the deck type where corporate mush creeps in
hardest. "Leverage synergies to unlock engagement" is not a plan. Say what you
will actually do, who does it and what it costs.

---

## Common failures

- **Objectives without baselines.** Then nobody can judge the target.
- **A tactics list with no strategy above it.** Activity is not a plan.
- **Budget that contradicts the stated priorities.**
- **Personas built from demographics.** See `campaign.md` on writing real ones.
- **KPIs with no check dates.** Measured once at the end is not measurement.
- **No named owners.** Then it is a wish list.
