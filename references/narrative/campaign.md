# Campaign Presentations

One creative idea, pitched. Launches, brand work, seasonal campaigns, and the
review afterwards. The audience is a client or a team deciding whether to back it.

The test is whether someone can see the campaign in their head when you finish.
Abstract strategy nobody can picture does not get approved.

**Density: 20 to 40 words a slide.** Show more than you tell. The creative
should occupy most of the pixels.

---

## The sequence

Eight to fifteen slides.

| # | Slide | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title** | Campaign name, client, the one-line idea |
| 2 | **The brief** | What was asked for, in their words |
| 3 | **The tension** | What makes this hard. No tension, no campaign |
| 4 | **The audience** | Who specifically. Real people |
| 5 | **The idea** | One sentence. Everything hangs from it |
| 6 | **The work** | The actual creative, large. Several slides |
| 7 | **The plan** | Channels, timing, budget |
| 8 | **The numbers** | What it costs and what it returns |
| 9 | **Risks** | What could go wrong and the response |
| 10 | **The ask** | Approval, budget, or the next step |

For a post-campaign review, replace 7 to 9 with what shipped, what the numbers
did, what worked, and what did not.

---

## Show the work

The single largest difference between a campaign deck that lands and one that
does not.

Do not describe the creative. Put it on screen at full size. Real copy in the
real language. Real placements in a real device frame. A push notification shown
inside a phone is worth three slides describing messaging strategy.

Use the device mockup and flip card in `components/interactive.md`. In a
PowerPoint deck, use full-bleed images of the creative instead.

**Never translate campaign copy into the deck language.** The audience has to
judge the actual words. Put a translation underneath in small type. See
`writing/languages.md`.

---

## The idea, in one sentence

If the campaign idea takes a paragraph, there is no idea yet.

> ✗ A multi-channel awareness campaign leveraging local partnerships
> ✓ Free rides on the worst day of the week, so the city tries us at the moment
>   it is most annoyed with the bus.

The one-sentence version is what people repeat after you leave the room. Write
it before anything else, and put it on its own slide.

---

## The audience slide

Demographics are not an audience. "Women 25 to 40, urban" is a census category
and tells you nothing about what to make.

Write people. What they do on a Tuesday, what annoys them, what they use instead
of you, what would make them switch. One quote in their own voice does more than
a table of attributes.

Two personas maximum. Three or more and none of them are real. Use the tab
switcher so both fit on one slide without competing.

---

## The tension

The slide most often skipped, and the deck then fails.

Something has to be in the way. A behaviour that resists, a budget that is too
small, a moment that only lasts three weeks, a competitor who got there first.
Without tension there is no reason the idea is clever rather than obvious.

> The bus is bad, but people are used to bad. Nobody switches transport because
> of an advert. They switch because of one specific annoying morning.

---

## Numbers

Campaign decks lose credibility on arithmetic more often than on creative.

- Show the funnel: reach, engagement, conversion, revenue, with real numbers at
  each step. Label the drop between stages, not only the stages.
- Name the assumption behind every projection and label it as an assumption.
- Show cost per outcome, not only total spend.
- Reporting on a finished campaign, show what missed as well as what worked. A
  review that only reports wins gets believed once.

---

## Voice

Closest to speech of any deck type. Contractions, short sentences, some rhythm.
Still no filler, still nothing said twice.

---

## Common failures

- **Describing the creative instead of showing it.**
- **Demographics standing in for an audience.**
- **No tension**, so the idea reads as arbitrary.
- **A channel list with no idea behind it.** Instagram is not a campaign.
- **Projections with hidden assumptions.**
- **A review that reports only the wins.**
